# AI tells to avoid

These patterns are not wrong grammar. They are statistically common in model text, so a page full of them reads generic. One or two is fine. Clusters are the problem.

## Sentence templates
- **Contrast flip:** "X isn't Y. It's Z." / "It's not about X, it's about Y." / "Not literal death. Metaphorical death." Max one per article, and only if it carries real information.
- **Fake reassurance one-liners:** "It didn't." "Neither." "And that's okay." "You're not alone."
- **Rule of three:** "courage, wisdom, and clarity", "evolve, adapt, and grow". Use two items, or four, or one. Lists of three are fine only when there really are three things.
- **Mirrored pairs:** "In the positive state... In the negative state..." repeated across every section.
- **Aphorism endings:** a last line that restates the paragraph as a quotable maxim ("Voluntary transformation is always less painful than forced destruction."). Cut it; the paragraph already said it.
- **Rhetorical question then answer:** "So what does this mean? It means..."
- **Colon reveals:** "It describes something far more useful: your relationship with..."
- **"Whether you're X or Y"** openers.
- **Second-person mind reading:** "Your first reaction was probably..." Use sparingly.

## Vocabulary
Flag and usually replace: delve, tapestry, testament, beacon, landscape (figurative), realm, navigate (figurative), journey (figurative), embark, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, foster, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, pivotal, crucial, vital, multifaceted, holistic, nuanced, intricate, profound, remarkable, genuinely, truly, deeply, ultimately, essentially, furthermore, moreover, additionally, in conclusion, it's worth noting, importantly, in today's fast-paced world, ever-evolving, game-changer, at its core, plays a key role, a wide range of, serves as, stands as.

## Clichéd images
Caterpillar and butterfly, phoenix from ashes, seasons changing, double-edged sword, tip of the iceberg, roller coaster, puzzle pieces, north star, compass, "at a cellular level". Replace with an image from the topic's own world, or with a plain fact.

## Structure
- Hook, then "The short answer" box, then table, then every item in fixed order, then FAQ, then "The Bottom Line". This exact skeleton is the giveaway of SEO model output.
- Every section the same length and same internal shape.
- Bolded lead-in on every list item or paragraph ("**Resilience under pressure.** Nothing breaks...").
- Headings like "Understanding X", "The Power of X", "Final Thoughts", "Key Takeaways", "The Bottom Line", "Conclusion".
- A conclusion that summarizes everything already said.
- Disclaimer blocks pasted in generic form. If a disclaimer is needed (health, money, legal), write it in one plain sentence in the site's voice where it is relevant.

## Tone
- Perfect balance on every point; never committing to a view.
- Hedge stacks: "may potentially", "can often", "tends to generally".
- Everything slightly dramatic; every sentence trying to be a highlight.
- Generic scenarios ("Imagine a manager who...") instead of a real example.

## Advisory / thought-leadership cadence (high-signal, avoid hard)
- **Hook and pivot opening:** a blunt contrarian line ("The honest answer is plain.", "Here's the truth nobody tells you.") then an instant pivot ("Most guides skip the uncomfortable numbers."). Both halves are tells. Never open by announcing honesty or attacking "most guides".
- **Performed candour:** "honestly", "the honest answer", "let's be real", "here's the thing", "the truth is", "nobody talks about".
- **Self-aware hedge tags:** "which is my opinion and yours may differ", "your mileage may vary", "take this with a grain of salt". A model inserts these to sound humble. A human states the opinion and moves on.
- **Mini Q&A fragments:** "Boring? Yes. It's also how most..." / "Terrifying? Absolutely." / "The catch?" Max one per article, ideally zero.
- **Ranking disclaimer framing:** "These are ranked by X" followed by a hedge. Just rank them, or explain the ranking with a reason drawn from data.

## Stats as decoration
- Dropping a big-name stat (Bankrate, FTC, Pew, Statista) into a paragraph whose conclusion was already decided, so the number "proves" a tidy moral.
- Every stat pointing the same way.
Fix: let at least one data point complicate the argument. Say what the number does NOT show (sample, year, country). Use local, niche or primary data where possible, not the most-quoted national headline figure.

## Neat moral arc
Problem, scary stat, reassuring reframe, "focus on skills instead" lesson. If the outline fits that arc, break it: lead with the practical answer, put the nuance where it applies, end on a specific action.

## Locale leaks and mixed signals
- "every rupee or dollar", "colour/color" mixing, US examples on an India-targeted page. Pick ONE target market and use its currency, spelling, examples, laws and platforms throughout.

## Shipping unfinished drafts
- Any `[ADD YOUR EXAMPLE]`, `[insert ...]`, `TODO`, `XX%` left in a published piece is the loudest tell of all.
