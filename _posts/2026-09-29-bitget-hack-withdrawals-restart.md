Bitget reopened Ethereum withdrawals at 08:00 UTC on September 29, two days after restarting Bitcoin, following a $387.5 million theft from the exchange's hot wallets on September 24. USDT withdrawals are scheduled for September 30, and every remaining asset by October 2.

The short version: the money is already gone, and what is left is whether the exchange can walk customers back to full access one network at a time. Bitcoin on Tuesday went as scheduled. Ethereum this morning did too.

## How much did the Bitget hack actually take?

About $387.5 million, up from Bitget's first estimate of $351.6 million.

Bitget's security systems flagged unauthorized transfers from some of its hot wallets at 18:31 UTC on September 24. Withdrawals were paused on the spot. Trading and deposits stayed open, which is why the order book kept printing bitcoin and ether futures fills while the exit was locked.

The estimate moved the next day after Bitget traced further transactions tied to the original incident, including holdings in Zcash and TRON. The company was clear that the revision came from identifying more of the same theft, not from additional unauthorized transfers.

Either way it is the largest exchange hack of 2026 so far, a title both the Financial Times and Fortune gave it.

A hot wallet is an online wallet that holds funds for live trading. Cold storage, kept offline, held the rest. Bitget says the cold wallets stayed intact.

## How did the attackers get in?

Bitget's root-cause analysis says the attacker exploited a zero-day in a third-party security product to steal internal network credentials, then used a valid identity to reach key internal management systems. A zero-day means a flaw the vendor has not patched yet.

That detail explains the delay before anyone noticed. Bitget's own systems signed the transfers, so the theft looked like legitimate traffic at the wallet layer. Its reconciliation process eventually flagged a large discrepancy, and the risk controls blocked user withdrawal requests.

Mandiant and SlowMist are assisting with the investigation. Bitget put a bounty of up to 5% on the table for anyone who helps freeze or recover the stolen funds.

On attribution, Chen has said preliminary IP and VPN evidence looks consistent with a North Korean group. That remains unproven, so treat it as a lead.

## When can you get your money out of Bitget?

Bitget is reopening withdrawals one asset at a time, 08:00 UTC each day, after each chain clears its own security checks.

| Date, 08:00 UTC | What reopens |
| --- | --- |
| September 28 | BTC on the bitcoin network |
| September 29 | ETH on Ethereum, BSC, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism |
| September 30 | USDT on Ethereum, BSC, Solana, Tron |
| October 2 | Remaining tokens, fiat rails, P2P |

The staggered schedule matters because assets come back on different networks. Someone pulling ether out on Ethereum has a different clock than someone waiting on BSC. Check which network your balance sits on before you queue anything.

USDT is the one to watch tomorrow. Most people park stablecoins on an exchange between trades, so the stablecoin exit is what the largest share of idle balances depends on.

## What if your crypto is still sitting on Bitget?

Bitget says customer balances are accurate and that its User Protection Fund will cover the financial impact. The fund held more than $464 million at the time of the breach, per the company. A User Protection Fund is an exchange-run reserve built to absorb exactly this kind of shortfall.

An accurate balance is a number on a screen. A working withdrawal is money landing in a wallet address you control. Bitcoin reopening on September 28 is the first hard evidence for the second, and ether the next day is more of it.

If you use an exchange, this is the argument for holding anything you cannot afford to lose in self-custody. A hot wallet is convenient right up until someone finds an unpatched flaw in a vendor's product.

## What this means in India

Bitget stopped opening new accounts to Indian residents from 4 PM IST on February 6, 2026, citing local regulatory requirements. Existing Indian accounts were left alone. If you signed up before then, the phased restart applies to you the same as it does to everyone else.

The tax rules have not moved. Income from transferring a virtual digital asset is taxed at a flat 30% under Section 115BBH, and 1% TDS applies on the consideration under Section 194S, deducted at source by the platform handling the transfer. Selling or swapping during this window triggers both, and the TDS you already paid should show up on Form 26AS so you can claim it back rather than pay twice.

## What to watch next

- USDT on September 30 at 08:00 UTC, across Ethereum, BSC, Solana and Tron.
- October 2 for remaining tokens, fiat and P2P. That closes the restart.
- Whether the User Protection Fund actually gets drawn down, and by how much.
- The formal post-incident report Bitget says is due this week.

## Bottom line

The theft happened in minutes and the funds are already in attacker-controlled wallets. Everything since has been administrative.

Bitcoin on September 28 and Ethereum on September 29 both went live on schedule. USDT tomorrow matters most, because that is where most idle balances sit. If it slips, the October 2 date is the one to start doubting.
