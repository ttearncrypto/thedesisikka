India's Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has issued comprehensive guidance on implementing the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), expanding tax reporting obligations for crypto-assets in line with global transparency standards.

## What changed

The CBDT guidance clarifies how Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Providers (RCASPs) must collect and report information on crypto-asset transactions. The move aligns India's domestic framework with the OECD CARF, which aims to close gaps in cross-border tax reporting for digital assets.

Under the updated approach, relevant crypto-asset service providers will need to follow new reporting rules covering transactions in scope, with the goal of improving visibility into crypto activity for tax purposes.

## Why it matters

Stricter reporting increases the likelihood that crypto transactions surface in tax assessments. For users and platforms operating in India, compliance expectations are rising as the country moves closer to automatic exchange of crypto-related tax information with other jurisdictions under CARF.

## What to watch

- **Implementation timeline:** Platforms will need to assess systems and processes to meet their reporting obligations as the framework takes effect.
- **Scope of coverage:** Details in the CBDT guidance define which entities and transactions fall in scope; providers are expected to review those definitions carefully.
- **Global alignment:** As more countries adopt CARF, India's approach signals closer coordination on crypto tax transparency.
