Ethereum trades near $2,480 on October 9, 2026, days after the network's next big upgrade cleared its Sepolia test fork. Wall Street's 12-month targets run from Citi's $3,028 to VanEck's $6,000. Getting there depends on one level that has rejected every rally this month: $2,750, and then the round number above it.

## Where is Ethereum trading right now?

ETH sat around $2,480 on October 9, down from roughly $2,700 on October 7. That's about half its all-time high of $4,946 from August 2025.

The retreat came fast. CoinDCX had ETH at $2,697.6 on October 7, with the 14-day RSI at 59.7. Within two days, spot had given back most of that week's gains. Prediction markets tracked the slide in real time: Polymarket's frontrunner for the October 9 close sat in the $2,400 to $2,500 band.

So the setup is simple. ETH is holding above its long-term trend lines but can't get out of its own way below $2,750.

## What do the institutions expect from ETH?

The 12-month calls are all higher than today's price, and all over the map:

- **Citi:** raised its ether target to $3,028 from $2,240 on October 1, 2026, citing stronger market activity.
- **Standard Chartered:** cut its 2026 target roughly in half to $4,000, but kept a $40,000 long-term call.
- **Bernstein:** $5,500.
- **VanEck:** $6,000.

Citi's update is the interesting one. It lifted ether by about 35% in a single revision and still sees more upside in bitcoin, where it now targets $113,000. Translation: the bank likes crypto broadly, and treats ETH as the second horse.

Treat these as 12-month views. Citi's number sits about 22% above the current price. That's a real gap and a modest one at the same time.

## What has to happen for ETH to reach $3,000?

Technically, ETH has to break a ceiling that's already turned it back three times.

As of the October 7 close, the daily exponential moving averages (EMAs) stacked in bullish order: the 20-day at $2,655, the 50-day at $2,500, the 100-day at $2,329 and the 200-day at $2,292. The 100-day sat $36 above the 200-day, a "golden cross" that has kept widening for weeks.

An exponential moving average is a running average that weights recent prices more heavily, so it reacts faster than a simple average.

That structure supports the trend. The price action doesn't yet. ETH has been rejected below $2,750 at $2,722, $2,747.9 and again at $2,724 in early October. CoinDCX's October model puts ETH at $2,950, inside a $2,655 to $3,100 range, and says a clean break above $2,800 is what unlocks the $3,000 zone.

The short version: close a day above $2,750 on decent volume, and $3,000 comes back into play. Close below $2,500, and the 100-day and 200-day averages near $2,300 are the next stop.

## What is Glamsterdam, and can it move the price?

Glamsterdam is Ethereum's next hard fork. A hard fork is a backwards-incompatible upgrade that every node has to adopt.

The Sepolia testnet forked on October 6, 2026 at 13:53 UTC, at epoch 353,024. Mainnet is targeted for Q4 2026, but the Ethereum Foundation still hasn't confirmed a date.

The upgrade brings native proposer-builder separation and block-level access lists. Proposer-builder separation (PBS) is a change that splits the job of ordering blocks from the job of building them, so validators don't have to run the most profitable builder software themselves. Block-level access lists let transactions declare which parts of the chain they touch, which opens the door to parallel execution.

Do upgrades move price on their own? Rarely. The Merge in 2022, Dencun in 2024 and Pectra in 2025 all landed without a lasting rally. Glamsterdam changes how the chain handles demand over months, not how it trades on the day.

## What is the bear case for ETH?

It's the level directly under the price.

If ETH loses $2,500, the 50-day EMA and the psychological line go together. Below that, support sits near $2,300, where the 100-day and 200-day EMAs have bunched up. A close under $2,400 would open the low $2,000s, and the CoinGecko prediction market already prices $2,400 support as more likely than not to hold through October.

The other risk is attention. Citi's own note hands the bigger upside to bitcoin. If ETF flows keep favouring BTC, ETH can drift lower without anything breaking.

## The bottom line

Ethereum's chart is bullish on trend and stuck in range on price. The moving averages point up, the $2,700 to $2,750 zone keeps capping rallies, and the market wants a reason to break it.

That reason could be a Glamsterdam mainnet date, a pick-up in ETF demand, or simply a bitcoin move that drags ETH along. Watch $2,750 for the upside and $2,500 for the downside. Everything between those two lines is noise.
