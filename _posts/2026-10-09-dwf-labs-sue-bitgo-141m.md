Two companies tied to crypto market maker DWF Labs are suing BitGo for $141 million. They claim the custodian sold Falcon Finance and ESPORTS tokens while the tokens were still locked, then let the prices slide. The case landed in London's High Court around October 2, 2026, and BitGo hasn't publicly answered yet.

## Who is suing whom?

The plaintiffs are DWF Maas and Falcon Digital, both linked to DWF Labs. BitGo is the defendant, a US custody firm that holds crypto for institutions and runs a regulated trust business.

The Financial Times and Law360 both reported the filing. CoinDesk followed on October 9. Nobody has published a full copy of the claim yet, so the specifics still come from what the plaintiffs' side is telling reporters.

## What is BitGo accused of?

DWF says BitGo broke over-the-counter agreements that put its FF and ESPORTS tokens under lock-up. A token lock-up is a contract that stops a holder from selling for a set period, usually so a pile of new supply doesn't hit the market all at once.

According to the claim, BitGo released or sold those tokens early. DWF argues that the early selling pushed the prices down and drained the value of the tokens still sitting in custody.

The FT quotes DWF pointing to the "sale of its FF" tokens as the trigger. That's the plaintiffs' wording, not a finding.

## Why $141 million?

That figure is the damages DWF is asking for. It's a claim, not a court order and not money in hand. It's meant to cover the drop in value DWF says it suffered when the tokens were sold ahead of schedule.

If the token prices had held, the argument goes, the locked holdings would still be worth far more. Whether a judge accepts that math is a different question.

## What does BitGo say?

Nothing yet. As of October 9, the custodian had not issued a public statement on the suit. The allegations are untested.

That matters. A $141 million lawsuit headline and a $141 million court ruling are two very different things. Right now this is one side's version of events, filed but not proven.

## What else is BitGo fighting in court?

BitGo is busy on the legal front:

- It's still tangled with Galaxy Digital, which walked away from a $1.2 billion deal to buy the company back in 2022. The two sides have been arguing over that termination for years.
- A federal securities class action filed in June 2026 is running in parallel. It targets BitGo's public statements around its stock listing.

Stack a token-lockup claim on top of those, and the custodial side of crypto's institutional stack starts to look legally crowded.

## What to watch next

Keep an eye on three things:

1. **BitGo's formal response.** A filed defense will lay out its version and may name the exact contract terms.
2. **The court documents.** The lock-up schedule, the sale dates, and the token amounts will decide whether the $141 million number holds up.
3. **Other holders.** Falcon Finance and ESPORTS had more locked holders than DWF. If the claim has legs, expect copycat filings.

For anyone using a custodian, the practical takeaway doesn't change: read what your lock-up or custody agreement says happens if the provider moves or lends your assets. The fine print is where the risk lives.

## The bottom line

DWF Labs affiliates want $141 million from BitGo over tokens they say were sold while locked. BitGo hasn't responded, no court has ruled, and the case is young. What matters is whether a custody agreement that promised "don't sell until date X" can be enforced when someone breaks it.
