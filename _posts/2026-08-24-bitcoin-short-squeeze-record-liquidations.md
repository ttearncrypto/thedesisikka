A bet against bitcoin just became one of the most expensive trades of 2026.

In a single 24-hour window this week, roughly $3 billion in crypto short positions got force-closed. Shorts made up about 92% of that damage. By most counts it was the largest short-side wipeout since 2021.

Price did what you'd expect when that many sellers are forced to become buyers. Bitcoin climbed from around $63,000 to nearly $79,500 before settling near $77,000, its first week above that level since May and a gain of more than 20%.

Here's how it happened, minus the jargon.

## How $3 billion evaporated

The fuse was lit on Wednesday, when the US Treasury said it would double the size of its liquidity-supporting bond buybacks, from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation starting September 9.

Translation for everyone who doesn't read bond desks: more dollars getting pushed into the system. The 10-year yield dipped about six basis points and the dollar softened against other majors. Bitcoin treated all of that as a green light and broke through resistance it had been pinned under for six weeks.

Then the mechanics took over.

Shorts are bets that price falls. A trader borrows coins, sells them, and plans to buy back cheaper. When price rises instead, the loss grows until the exchange force-closes the position. That closing is a purchase. It pushes price higher, which triggers the next round of closings.

That chain reaction is a squeeze. CoinGlass data put the damage between $2.7 and $3 billion depending on the window measured, with roughly 172,000 accounts liquidated in a day. Ethereum shorts took another $1.13 billion of pain on the way up.

## Why squeeze rallies deserve suspicion

Forced buying runs out by definition. Every short that gets closed is one less buyer who must buy. Once the last over-leveraged bear is gone, price needs ordinary demand to hold the line.

That's why ETF flows matter more than candles right now. On that front, the week actually delivered. US spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in about $1.9 billion and ethereum funds added roughly $700 million, the best combined week since October per SoSoValue data. BlackRock's funds alone absorbed around 70% of the bitcoin side.

One strong week is one data point. The week before showed net outflows. Watch whether inflows repeat next week; a single spike after a vertical candle is exactly what profit-taking looks like from the inside.

Sentiment flipped fast too. The Fear & Greed Index sits at 73, deep in greed territory after reading 31 just last week. Crowds arriving after the move starts is usually how squeezes end.

## The Washington backdrop

Policy noise stacked up around the Treasury announcement. President Trump hosted crypto industry leaders at the White House this week and publicly pushed Congress to pass the CLARITY Act, which would set clearer federal rules for digital assets. The SEC also floated a friendlier framework for token offerings midweek, with exemptions for smaller projects.

Nothing got signed. Markets priced the direction anyway, and in a market loaded with downside bets, mildly good news trades like great news.

## If you're holding from India

Your tax math didn't change with the candles. Gains still get taxed at a flat 30%, and every sale carries 1% TDS. Whipsaw weeks plus those fixed costs is how traders lose money twice: once on the trade, again on the churn.

If you SIP into bitcoin, keep the SIP running. If you trade levels instead of feelings, two numbers do all the work right now. Losing $77,000 opens the door to profit-taking. Reclaiming $80,000 extends the move. Between those lines, nobody knows anything, and pretending otherwise gets expensive.

And margin after a 20%-plus green week remains the fastest way to own none of the upside you called correctly.

## Bottom line

The squeeze told us positioning was lopsided and leverage was stacked on one side. The ETF flows tell us real money showed up while it happened. Next week separates those two stories: either inflows continue and price digests the jump, or they fade and the market hands back what the liquidation engine distributed.

You don't need to predict Monday. You need a position you can hold through either level breaking.

The squeeze is over. Now we find out who actually wants to own bitcoin at $77,000.
