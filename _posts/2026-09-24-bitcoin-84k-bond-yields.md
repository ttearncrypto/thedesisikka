Bitcoin fell about 2% to near $84,340 on September 24, wiping $444 million in long positions in 24 hours, after a rally built on record US ETF inflows ran into the highest Treasury yields since 2007. The 10-year yield touched 5.11%, and the market gave back a chunk of the week's gains.

A liquidation is when an exchange force-closes a margin position because the trader's collateral no longer covers the loss. Exchanges report it millions of dollars at a time, which is why you see it quoted in lump sums.

## What happened to the rally this week?

Monday looked like the opposite story. Spot bitcoin ETFs took in $999 million, their biggest single session since October 2025, and followed with $714.7 million on Tuesday, per Farside data. Roughly $1.7 billion crossed into the funds in two days, about enough to turn their 2026 net flows positive.

Price ran with it. Bitcoin climbed about 12% in five sessions, back above $86,000 for the first time since January, nearly erasing its losses for the year. Monday's move was a short squeeze: short liquidations touched $925 million that day against $168 million in long liquidations, per CoinGlass.

Then Wednesday reversed the tape.

## Why did the overnight trend suddenly flip?

The bond market moved first. S&P Global's flash composite PMI came in at 58.4 for September, up from 56.0 and the fastest reading in more than five years, and the 10-year Treasury yield jumped through 5.11%, its highest since 2007. Higher yields raise the cost of holding assets that pay nothing, and bitcoin sits at the front of that line.

Fed Governor Michael Barr added fuel in a Chicago speech the same day. "In my view, further policy adjustments may be needed to ensure that inflation falls to the target level in a timely manner," he said, in the wake of the Fed's first rate hike in three years and talk that markets now weight another move in October.

The result showed up in the derivatives books. Long positions worth $444 million were liquidated in 24 hours, the most since September 15, and about 77% of the roughly $580 million in total market liquidations that day, per CoinGlass. The heaviest batch of longs came undone around the PMI release.

## Does $84,000 decide where bitcoin goes next?

On-chain data says yes. Glassnode's weekly report flags the $84,000 to $85,000 band as the largest concentration of long-term holder supply below the current price, which tends to act as support. Bitcoin also reclaimed the True Market Mean near $77,000, the average on-chain acquisition cost of coins actively traded in secondary markets.

The next major resistance is the mean MVRV price at $96,700. MVRV compares the market value of coins to the price their holders paid, and this level marks where the average holder's profit returns to its long-term norm. Glassnode calls $95,000 to $97,000 the first real test if the bounce holds.

Hold above $84,000 and the path to $96,700 stays open, per the report. Lost it, and $77,000 comes back into view. Dealer positioning on Deribit already built heavy positive gamma around the $95,000 strikes, which tends to brake price moves near that zone.

The notable part: this selloff has not produced the profit-taking of past tops. Weekly net realized profit sits near $5.1 billion, a fraction of the 2024 and 2025 peaks, and closer to late-2023 levels. Holder exits have been mild so far.

## Who is still buying the dip?

The flow data and the tape are telling different stories. ETFs pulled in close to $1 billion in a single session right before the drop, and funding on OKX flipped negative overnight, meaning traders were paying to hold short positions rather than long ones. That combination usually sets up a squeeze the other way.

Altcoin data points the same direction. Glassnode says 72.5% of tracked altcoins outperformed bitcoin over the past week, yet open interest in altcoin perpetual futures barely moved in 30 days. The rally leaned on spot buying, which makes a sudden wave of forced selling less likely.

## What should holders watch next?

- Wednesday's ETF flow number, which lands Thursday. It is the first read on whether institutional buyers stayed in during the dip.
- The 10-year yield. It climbed at the same time as oil and sits near a two-decade high; another leg up is the main risk to a no-yield asset.
- The $84,000 line and the $95,000 to $97,000 zone on any bounce, per the Glassnode levels above.

Indian holders do not get to skip this either way. The flat 30% tax on gains and the 1% TDS on transfers over ₹50,000 apply whether bitcoin is at $77,000 or $96,000, and the new VDA reporting rules that started April 1 changed the paperwork, not the rates.

## Bottom line

Bitcoin's week is a contest between two charts. Fund flows say institutions were buying through Monday; the bond market says the cost of sitting on a no-yield asset just went up to a 2007 high. The next tell is Thursday's ETF print and whether the 10-year yield holds at 5% or pushes higher. Until then, the $84,000 band is the pivot, with $96,700 above and $77,000 below.
