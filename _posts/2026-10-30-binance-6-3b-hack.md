Binance confirmed a $6.3 billion theft from its hot wallets on October 29, 2026, the largest crypto hack on record. The exchange says user funds are safe, has set aside more than $6.34 billion of company money to cover losses, and has frozen about $3 billion of the stolen assets.

Withdrawals are still paused. Deposits are open.

## What happened at Binance?

The attack started at 20:05 UTC on October 29. TRM Labs says about $6.36 billion left Binance wallets across 10 blockchains, most of it within roughly 20 minutes. Arkham put the haul at $6.01 billion across 9 chains.

The haul included bitcoin, ether, USDT, USDC and smaller tokens. Binance pegged the total impact at around $6.3 billion.

The February 2025 Bybit hack took $1.5 billion. TRM says this one is about four times larger.

Bitcoin fell more than 4% after the news, according to Nikkei Asia. The FUN token dropped about 90%.

## How did the attacker get in?

Binance's first explanation pointed to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop. The company said that access let an attacker reach a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. It found no evidence of a wallet private key leak.

The story has since widened. Co-CEO Richard Teng said a follow-up investigation found additional points of breach. He described a complex operation that did not rely on one employee's permissions or a single click.

Teng also gave the technical route. The attacker abused EIP-7702, an Ethereum upgrade that lets a regular wallet hand control of its execution to smart-contract code. That delegation gave on-chain approval over a hot wallet and skipped Binance's normal approval controls.

Teng said the attackers also used a self-destruct mechanism that wiped tooling and traces as they moved.

## Is user money safe?

Binance says yes. It is paying from company funds.

The numbers, as of October 30:

- More than $6.34 billion reserved in company funds.
- $5 billion already held in the exact tokens that were taken, including USDT, USDC, BTC, ETH and BNB.
- The remaining roughly $1.3 billion is being bought through public markets and over-the-counter desks.
- About $3 billion frozen on-chain with help from industry partners, kept separate from the reserve.

The Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) is Binance's emergency insurance pool, funded by trading fees. It holds around $1 billion to $1.2 billion.

CoinDesk reviewed Binance's October 1 proof of reserves and found that Binance 14, the hot wallet at the centre of the attack, was part of the assets backing customer balances. About 204,500 ETH was taken, roughly 5.6% of Binance's ETH reserves, which left ETH backing near 94% until the exchange replaces it.

Proof of reserves is a public report that compares the assets an exchange holds with what customers are owed. Binance's next snapshot is due November 1, with a full report promised by November 2.

## What has been frozen?

Stablecoin issuers moved fast. Tether froze more than $1.567 billion in USDT. Circle froze about $823 million in USDC. Ripple froze around $200 million, and World Liberty Financial froze $27 million in USD1.

A freeze stops the money from moving. Recovery usually needs a court order or seizure warrant before issuers burn and reissue the tokens. Bitcoin and ether are harder, because no issuer can freeze them. Investigators have to catch them at an exchange or another service.

Arkham tracked a Tron wallet linked to the hack sending millions in TRX to new addresses through Friday.

## Who is behind the attack?

TRM Labs and Chainalysis both attribute the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed unit also tracked as Lazarus Group and APT38. TRM says it made the call with high confidence. Chainalysis says a high degree of confidence.

Binance has not confirmed the attribution. The exchange says it is working with law enforcement to identify the attackers. The US Secret Service says it is investigating, and the FBI says it is aware of the incident.

North Korea has a long record of crypto theft. The Bybit hack in February 2025 was also blamed on TraderTraitor.

## What should users watch next?

The withdrawal restart is the first test. Binance is targeting Sunday, November 1, after rebuilding and testing the service. The exchange has said withdrawals stay off until testing passes.

The proof of reserves report comes next, due Monday, November 2. Check whether the ETH and altcoin ratios return to 100% and whether the $5 billion in matching tokens shows up in the published addresses.

Then watch the frozen funds. Freezing is the first step. Recovery is the second. Track whether issuers burn and reissue the stablecoins, and whether law enforcement seizes any of the bitcoin or ether.

For users, the practical steps are boring. Log in through the official app or website, check balances and withdrawal history, and review active sessions. Binance says it will never ask for a password, OTP or seed phrase.

## Bottom line

Binance has the money set aside and a public timetable for withdrawals and proof of reserves.

The open questions are whether the November 1 restart holds, whether the reserve report shows full backing, and how much of the $3 billion freeze turns into actual recovery. Until withdrawals work again, treat "your funds are safe" as a company statement that still needs the proof of reserves to back it.
